01 / Research question

Many teams.
How many winners?

Basketball is played across Asia, but the championship trophy has often returned to a small group of national teams. This project measures how concentrated those wins have been.

The central question

How concentrated are FIBA Asia Cup titles, and did the inclusion of Oceania change competitive balance?

We compare champions across four historical periods and use the Herfindahl–Hirschman Index (HHI).

Why it matters

A tournament can include many national teams while a small number win most editions. Tracking champions over time helps us see whether success is becoming more widely shared or more concentrated.

03 / Key findings

Four patterns
stand out.

The results point to different periods of dominance rather than one continuous story.

01

Early variety

From 1960 to 1973, the Philippines, Japan and South Korea shared the titles.

02

China’s long run

China won 14 of 16 editions between 1975 and 2005, making this a highly concentrated era.

03

Iran challenges the pattern

Iran won three of five editions from 2007 to 2015, while China won the other two.

04

Australia’s recent streak

Australia won the 2017, 2022 and 2025 editions — all three tournaments in the latest period.

06 / Method

How the study
works.

This is a descriptive study based on historical tournament results, not a prediction of future winners.

01 · Collect

Record the champion of each completed FIBA Asia Cup edition from 1960 to 2025.

02 · Group

Organize tournament editions into four periods to compare changes over time.

03 · Measure

Calculate title shares and use HHI to describe how concentrated the championships are.

04 · Interpret

Compare the periods and discuss what the results suggest about competitive balance.
